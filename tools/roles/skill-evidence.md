# Evidence for each sentence of the real skills

Skills carry no comments in their own text, so that each placebo twin can match its
real skill exactly in length; this file holds, for every sentence of every real skill,
the failure it prevents and its evidence. `rolemap.py check` fails when a skill's
sentences and this list disagree. "The earlier method" is the owner's issue-tree
method from the earlier system (a private design record), which this skill rewrites.

## decomposition-method
- The tree breaks the question asked into the sub-questions whose answers, together, answer it. <!-- prevents: a tree built for a bigger question than the one asked (run 1 turned "is the U.S. on track" into "which measures to fund"); evidence: decision D1, run 1 diagnosis cause C1, tree-skill diagnosis of 27 October (books' trees as the referee) -->
- Your reasoning about the question (its terms, the working answer and its rival, why you chose the top cut) goes in frame.md; the tree holds only the questions. <!-- prevents: reasoning, rules and output definitions written into node statements (Q0's "Output: ...", "Division principle:" in Q1's and Q2's nodes); evidence: tree-skill diagnosis of 27 October, "The evidence" and lines 3 and 7 -->
- Ideas. <!-- prevents: a tidy tree thinner than the ideas a consultant would raise; evidence: owner ruling 48, tree check 2 owner judgment (principle 3) -->
- Start from framer/ideas.md and add your own: every driver, factor and sub-question an expert would raise. <!-- prevents: a lean tree missing drivers the plain model raised unprompted (11 to 16 nodes against 40 to 60); evidence: owner ruling 48, tree check 2 owner judgment (third case B and D, principles 3 and 4) -->
- Every idea either finds a place in the tree or is set aside with its reason in frame.md; none is dropped silently. <!-- prevents: good ideas lost between the idea list and the tree with no one able to see why; evidence: owner ruling 48, tree check 2 owner judgment (principle 3, "then prune") -->
- A placed idea is a node of its own, a child of the node it is a part or a cause of, never folded into another node's wording or settles_by. <!-- prevents: ideas "placed" by folding them into broader nodes, so a 61-line idea list became a 16-node tree (frame.md: demand drivers listed against two forecast nodes); evidence: tree check 3 airport run under 12ba6c5, orchestrator message of 27 October, owner ruling 48 (the plain tree's depth of ideas) -->
- Only set-aside ideas leave the tree. <!-- prevents: ideas that vanish without a recorded reason; evidence: owner ruling 48, tree check 2 owner judgment (principle 3) -->
- Structure. <!-- prevents: sibling groups that mix kinds and levels, the failure the owner believes most common; evidence: tree check 2 owner judgment (principles 1 and 2), owner ruling of 27 October to follow the books, BOOK-PRINCIPLES.md of 27 October -->
- Take the top cut from how the situation works, not from the question's wording: split a whole into its parts, trace the causes of one result, or sort like things into classes. <!-- prevents: a top cut copied from the question's words or the material's reporting lines, which leaves no way to judge completeness; evidence: BOOK-PRINCIPLES.md of 27 October: Minto 789-806 and 10618-10624, 6115; tree-skill diagnosis of 27 October ("Wrong nesting") -->
- Try two or three cuts, keep the most revealing, and say why in frame.md. <!-- prevents: the first cut kept untested, though the principle of division decides what the tree can see; evidence: BOOK-PRINCIPLES.md of 27 October: Conn and McLean 2683, 3220, 2989; Minto 3392-3399 -->
- Each parent's children come from one of those acts, are the same kind of question, and sit one step below it: answered together they answer the parent, and no two answer the same thing. <!-- prevents: a group that is mutually exclusive and complete yet mixes a measure of the whole, a split of it and one cause acting on it; evidence: BOOK-PRINCIPLES.md of 27 October: Minto 754-806, 3354-3368; Conn and McLean 2714-2724; tree check 2 owner judgment (principle 1) -->
- A factor that reaches the parent only through one child goes under that child; one acting through several goes inside each, as that child's own question. <!-- prevents: a specific factor raised to a primary branch beside the category it acts through, and a cross-cutting factor set as its own branch; evidence: BOOK-PRINCIPLES.md of 27 October: Howson 11695-11700, Minto 4208-4216, Conn and McLean 2712; tree check 2 owner judgment (principle 2) -->
- Test each group: one specific plural noun names it, and its members fall in one order (parts by structure, causes by time, classes by importance). <!-- prevents: a loose label passing for a group, hiding members with no single source; evidence: BOOK-PRINCIPLES.md of 27 October: Minto 783-788, 3074-3077, 3123-3160 -->
- More than five members usually hides a subgroup. <!-- prevents: long flat lists whose closer relations go unstated (a prompt, not a cap); evidence: BOOK-PRINCIPLES.md of 27 October: Minto 3230-3236, Howson 11700 -->
- Use the structure to find what the idea list missed. <!-- prevents: a sorted list taken as complete, though a list cannot show what is missing; evidence: BOOK-PRINCIPLES.md of 27 October: Minto 3616-3618, 5944-5946 -->
- Split a branch until evidence can settle it. <!-- prevents: vague leaves that drive no analysis, and trees with no deeper sub-branches; evidence: BOOK-PRINCIPLES.md of 27 October: Minto 5944-5950, Rasiel and Friga 1272, Conn and McLean 2691; tree check 2 owner judgment (q1) -->
- Prune. <!-- prevents: every idea kept because it was written down; evidence: tree check 2 owner judgment (principle 3, "not every idea is gold") -->
- Set aside only ideas whose every answer leaves the headline unchanged, each named in frame.md with its reason. <!-- prevents: irrelevant branches kept, hard decisive ones dropped, and set-asides nobody can audit; evidence: decision D5, run 1 diagnosis cause C5, tree check 2 owner judgment (principle 3) -->
- No branch rests on an unchecked premise: make the premise the question. <!-- prevents: a branch that assumes what it should ask (how to raise a limit the tree never asked whether it reaches); evidence: tree check 2 owner judgment (third case D 2.3, principle 5) -->
- No node asks for a definition, a method, a sensitivity or a work plan. <!-- prevents: branches that are not questions of the issue (how a term should be defined; whether two forecasts cross); evidence: tree check 2 owner judgment (q3 B, third case D 3, principle 5) -->
- Every node. <!-- prevents: node form left to chance once the tree grows; evidence: tree-skill diagnosis of 27 October, line 7 -->
- Each node is one short question with one idea, in plain words; how to answer it goes in settles_by. <!-- prevents: few fat nodes packing several ideas and how-to notes (median 37 to 51 words against 11 to 15), and nodes that list their own sub-branches; evidence: tree-skill diagnosis of 27 October, "Size and density"; tree check 2 owner judgment (q3 B) -->
- A parent does not list or summarise its children, and a node has no children or at least two. <!-- prevents: parents restating their children ("Children:" lines) and single children that add a level without dividing anything; evidence: tree-skill diagnosis of 27 October, "The evidence" and "Wrong nesting" -->
- The tree holds only sub-questions of the question asked: no option, rival, check or work plan is a node. <!-- prevents: a structure holding options, a rival, a check and a gate beside the real sub-questions (run 1's seven roots); evidence: decision D2, run 1 diagnosis causes C3 and C4 -->
- For a decision among named options, the top parts are what the choice turns on, asked of every option alike; for a question asking which options are best, the first part finds the candidates. <!-- prevents: top parts that are each one option's terms (Q1), and a list question whose tree never asks for the list (Q3); evidence: decision D2, v2 tree check notes (Q1, Q3) -->
- Keep one open_lane node for primary reading nobody planned. <!-- prevents: a tree that leaves no place for what nobody thought to ask; evidence: decision D2, contract section 2, tree-skill diagnosis of 27 October -->
- Only leaves need doubt, settles_by and flip_point. <!-- prevents: every node made a work package, so nodes are expensive and few; evidence: tree-skill diagnosis of 27 October, line 1; register/store.py defaults them to null -->
- Mark the one to three nodes the answer turns on as crux. <!-- prevents: effort spread evenly instead of on what decides the answer; evidence: the earlier method's ranking step, decision D5, tree-skill diagnosis of 27 October -->
- Leaves that rest on the same sources share one branch_id: a branch, not a leaf, is one worker's job. <!-- prevents: a finer tree starting one worker per leaf, multiplying workers that read the same sources; evidence: tree-skill diagnosis of 27 October, "Consequence to watch in the fix"; the earlier method's lines-of-evidence step -->
- When a check or a critique finds a gap, an overlap or a node doing two jobs, add, split, move or merge nodes; never fix a tree by adding words to a node. <!-- prevents: the critique round doubling node text instead of changing the structure (Q1: Q0 113 to 182 words, Q2 34 to 79, Q3.3 40 to 77); evidence: tree-skill diagnosis of 27 October, "The critique round" and lines 5 and 6 -->

## card-company-evaluation

- Use this card when the question is about a company; it says where to look and what to ask, and it holds no industry knowledge and no valuation method. <!-- prevents: a company lens applied to every question, and a card standing in for domain knowledge the user should attach; evidence: lessons 2.0, 2.0a and 2.2a -->
- For a pitch, the goal is insight the company's own management may lack, grounded in its record, not a price for the company. <!-- prevents: a pitch answered with a valuation or a summary of what is already public; evidence: lesson 2.2a, the owner's brief for this card -->
- Search the company's own published record for what the scoped question needs (its site and documents, product, pricing, terms and hiring pages, leadership interviews), and read whole every document you rely on; a sample of a page is not the page. <!-- prevents: findings drawn from a sampled page, without ordering every document a company publishes whatever the question; evidence: lesson 2.2a item 1, decision D1, Codex review of the v2 texts -->
- Where a change over time bears on the question, compare its statements through dated archived copies. <!-- prevents: missing the change that its press releases do not mention; evidence: lesson 2.2a item 1 and its invented example -->
- Where the question needs them, go on to its regulatory filings, the records any company leaves (registries, officer filings, secured-lending filings, court records, licences, patents and trademarks, government contracts), and the full disclosures of its public customers, suppliers, competitors and acquirers. <!-- prevents: a private company treated as having no record, and peers' disclosures left unread, without widening a narrow question into a full company study; evidence: lesson 2.2a items 2 to 4, decision D1, Codex review of the v2 texts -->
- Use official statistics at source and recompute from the files; use trade press and reviews as leads and as the market's voice. <!-- prevents: a write-up's figure taken in place of the data it summarises; evidence: lesson 2.2a items 5 to 7 -->
- Each of these is a prompt to look, not a quota: market definition and size, position against a named universe of competitors, where the profit is earned against peers' disclosed unit economics, customer and supplier concentration counted at the controlling group, channel, ownership and management, key contracts and regulatory exposure, technology and operating model. <!-- prevents: a checklist filled for its own sake, and concentration understated by counting subsidiaries separately; evidence: lesson 2.2a -->
- For any of these that turns on how the business works, model the mechanism from data (see card-mechanism) rather than quote someone's conclusion. <!-- prevents: insight borrowed from analysts instead of built; evidence: lessons 1.2 and 2.11 -->
- Record which lenses you ran, which returned something, and which you skipped and why. <!-- prevents: silent gaps in coverage; evidence: lesson 2.2a -->
- Tier the company's description of itself as interested_party until a count or a primary source confirms it. <!-- prevents: a company's own count of itself carried as fact (an invented example, labelled as invented: a firm's site says "more than 300 branches" and the memo repeats it as a measured count); evidence: lesson 2.4 -->
- State an inference as a finding with its basis and confidence, separate from what was measured, and make it a question only where the evidence in hand cannot carry it. <!-- prevents: claiming knowledge the research lacks, without turning defensible insights into questions; evidence: lesson 7 rule 7, blind review of these texts -->
- When the client's own questions are known, map each to answered, out of scope by agreement, or unaddressed. <!-- prevents: most of a client's questions never assigned; evidence: lesson 2.8 -->

## card-decision

- Use this card when the question itself asks whether to act or which option to take; it holds method, not an industry's economics or a valuation model. <!-- prevents: a decision lens applied to every question, including one whose purpose line merely mentions a decision (run 1 loaded it as the main card for a fact question), and a generic framework filling a specialist gap; evidence: lessons 2.0 and 2.0a, decision D2, run 1 diagnosis "Where it came from" -->
- Take the options the question or the user names: for "whether to do X", doing it and not doing it; for "A or B", A and B. <!-- prevents: options invented at framing that the user never posed (run 1's O1 to O5), without losing the alternative a "whether" question already contains; evidence: decision D2 (options fixed at framing when the user names them), run 1 diagnosis point A5 -->
- Make the tree's top parts the criteria every option is compared on (what each offers, how certain it is, what it costs), never one option's own terms as a part, and find early which criterion could end the question. <!-- prevents: one nested tree per option, top parts that are each one option's terms (the v2 tree check's decision question), and effort spread evenly while the knock-out criteria wait; evidence: decision D2, run 1 diagnosis D-11, v2 tree check notes (Q1), P3 failure kind 12 -->
- If the question asks what to do and names no options, work the options out from the findings after the research, never before it. <!-- prevents: options placed before research shaping which nodes get researched; evidence: decision D2, run 1 diagnosis D-11 and point A7 (levers read off the drivers the research found) -->
- Take what the decider is trying to achieve from the question and task/; if the options cannot be ranked against it, say so as the first finding. <!-- prevents: options compared with each other instead of with the goal, without turning the purpose into research of its own; evidence: D1 principle 12, decision D1, blind review of the v2 texts -->
- Go to the primary material about the thing being decided: for a physical asset, the records of whoever grants its permits and connections; for a company, its record and its counterparties'; for a product, the people who would buy it. <!-- prevents: a decision made from articles about the sector; evidence: lesson 2.2 and its invented example -->
- Treat the materials of anyone selling the deal or the venture as advocacy, tiered interested_party, and look for what they leave out. <!-- prevents: a seller's figures taken as fact; evidence: lesson 2.4 -->
- Quantify the effect of each option with its assumptions and a range, and say at what value of the deciding fact the choice would flip. <!-- prevents: a comparison with no magnitude or break-even; evidence: D1 principle 12, P3 R2 -->
- Recommend an option only when the question asks for a recommendation, and then state what would have to happen for it to fail and whether any of it is already visible. <!-- prevents: a recommendation nobody asked for (run 1's spending headline), and one that has never been argued against; evidence: decision D2 (under-prescribe), run 1 diagnosis point A3, D1 principle 12, lesson 7 rule 12 -->
- Give the reason for any choice as how it meets the goal, with the real alternatives shown and straw men left out. <!-- prevents: a choice justified by knocking down options nobody proposed; evidence: D1 principle 12 -->

## card-market-sizing

- Use this card when the question asks how large a market or opportunity is; it holds method, not figures for any industry. <!-- prevents: a sizing lens forced onto questions that do not need it, and remembered figures standing in for sources; evidence: lessons 2.0 and 2.0a -->
- Define the market by the customer need and what substitutes for it, not by an industry label, starting from the user's own term, and state the definition before any number. <!-- prevents: a size that cannot be compared or checked because its scope is unstated; evidence: lessons 3.0 and 7 rule 4 -->
- Size it bottom-up from a funnel (the population that has the need, those who can be reached, those who adopt, at what price and frequency) and top-down from totals such as official statistics, sector filings or peers' revenues. <!-- prevents: a single route whose error nobody can see; evidence: lesson 2.2a "sized both top-down and bottom-up" -->
- Where adoption, access or awareness binds, put it in the funnel as its own stage rather than inside an adoption rate. <!-- prevents: the binding constraint hidden in one assumed percentage; evidence: the earlier method's sizing router, lesson 2.11 step 1 -->
- Reconcile the two routes, and check the share a known player's revenue implies; a large gap means a definition or an input is wrong, and is investigated rather than averaged. <!-- prevents: averaging away the error that would have shown the definition was wrong; evidence: lesson 2.11 step 4 -->
- Take official statistics from the source files and recompute; tier vendor and association totals as interested_party or secondary. <!-- prevents: a report's market size quoted as measured; evidence: lessons 2.2a item 5 and 2.4 -->
- Build the calculation in a script in analysis/ with each input labelled observed, estimated or assumed, and show which two or three inputs move the answer most and the range they imply. <!-- prevents: a point estimate with no basis or range; evidence: lesson 2.11, P3 R2 -->
- A narrow sizing question gets a short answer with its method, not a deck. <!-- prevents: a one-number task padded into a long deliverable; evidence: lesson 7D "When it applies", design step 13 -->

## card-mechanism
- Use this card when the answer turns on how something works or what a change would do; you build the analysis rather than look for a source that states the answer. <!-- prevents: hunting for a headline that states the answer; evidence: lesson 2.11, design step 7 -->
- Write the output as an equation of its drivers before collecting any number. <!-- prevents: inputs gathered with no structure to say which matter; evidence: lesson 2.11 step 1 -->
- Label each input observed, estimated or assumed, with its source and the basis it is on (the price actually paid or the list price, one period or another). <!-- prevents: an assumed input presented as sourced, and inputs on mismatched bases; evidence: lesson 2.11 step 2, lesson 3.0 on basis -->
- Compute it in a script in analysis/, and show the break-even and the range over the inputs that move it most. <!-- prevents: arithmetic done in prose, and a point answer with no sense of what moves it; evidence: lesson 2.11 step 3 -->
- For example, a price cut pays only if volume rises enough to replace the margin it gives up, and the script shows how much that is. <!-- prevents: a price change recommended without its break-even, the owner's named example of a mechanism question; evidence: lesson 2.11 and its invented example, the brief for this card -->
- Test the model against what was observed (past changes, reported results, peers), and investigate a large gap rather than averaging it away. <!-- prevents: a tidy model that history already contradicts; evidence: lesson 2.11 step 4 -->
- Say how competitors or customers could react, and whether the result holds if they do. <!-- prevents: a change that only works if nobody responds; evidence: lesson 2.11, D1 principle 12 -->
- Say when the method is specialist (pricing studies, actuarial or engineering models), use an attached study or ask for one, and otherwise label the method first-principles. <!-- prevents: a specialist question answered with a generic model presented as expert; evidence: lessons 2.0a and 2.11 step 5 -->
