# Evidence for each sentence of the real skills

Skills carry no comments in their own text, so that each placebo twin can match its
real skill exactly in length; this file holds, for every sentence of every real skill,
the failure it prevents and its evidence. `rolemap.py check` fails when a skill's
sentences and this list disagree. "The earlier method" is the owner's issue-tree
method from the earlier system (a private design record), which this skill rewrites.

## decomposition-method
- Think the frame and tree through in prose in frame.md first; enter the tree with tree_upsert only once the prose holds together. <!-- prevents: structure emitted before the thinking is done, the earlier method's "single most important rule"; evidence: the earlier method, "write the decomposition first, format last" -->
- Decompose the question asked. <!-- prevents: a tree built for a bigger question than the one asked (run 1 turned "is the U.S. on track" into "which measures to fund"); evidence: decision D1 and decision D2, run 1 diagnosis "In short" and cause C1 -->
- The tree breaks the question, in the user's words and read narrowly unless the scope says otherwise, into the sub-questions that together answer it. <!-- prevents: a thin request widened toward "the question behind the question"; evidence: decision D1 (narrow reading by default), run 1 diagnosis causes C2 and D-12 -->
- The purpose says what form and precision the answer needs, never what else to research. <!-- prevents: the purpose line used as licence to add research (run 1's "decide whether the target needs new measures" became five options and a lever tree); evidence: decision D1, run 1 diagnosis cause C2 and D-3 -->
- A sub-question that matters only to a broader decision stays out of the tree and goes in the closing note on what was not researched. <!-- prevents: unasked branches (about a third of run 1's worker spend and of its memo), and a reader never told what was left out; evidence: decision D1, run 1 diagnosis "In short" and node table (K1, K2, L1 to L4) -->
- Let the question's type pick the frame. <!-- prevents: every question made a choice between options, the root of run 1's tree; evidence: decision D2, run 1 diagnosis cause C1 and "Where it came from" -->
- A fact or forecast question gets an answer with its confidence. <!-- prevents: options and a recommendation hung on a question that asked for a fact; evidence: decision D2, run 1 diagnosis D-11 -->
- A "why" question gets a cause, separating the normal level from the causes of shortfall. <!-- prevents: remedies proposed before the cause is known; evidence: decision D2, the earlier method's tree-type router, run 1 diagnosis D-11 -->
- A decision question gets options: fixed now when the user named them, with the tree's sub-questions as the shared criteria every option is compared on and no option owning a subtree; otherwise worked out after the research, from the findings. <!-- prevents: options placed before research shaping which nodes get researched, and one nested tree per option (run 1's five trees under O1 to O5); evidence: decision D2, run 1 diagnosis "Options at the top", D-11 and point A5 -->
- Recommend nothing the user did not ask for. <!-- prevents: a headline ending in a spending recommendation nobody asked for (run 1); evidence: decision D2 (under-prescribe), run 1 diagnosis point A3 -->
- Hold a working answer and its rival in frame.md, never in the tree. <!-- prevents: anchoring on one answer, and a rival stored as a branch that duplicates another node's work (run 1's R1 under T); evidence: decision D2, run 1 diagnosis D-7 and point B6 -->
- State the working answer so that evidence could prove it wrong, and the strongest rival answer. <!-- prevents: a hypothesis that cannot fail, and a rival that is a straw man; evidence: design step 2, the earlier method step 2 -->
- While you build the tree, ask whether a sub-question exists whose answer would separate the two, and add it if it is missing. <!-- prevents: a tree with no node that could tell the working answer from its rival (run 1's level-against-slope flaw, found only by the framer); evidence: decision D2 (the rival as a challenge while the tree is built), run 1 framer/critique.md point 1 -->
- After the research, the rival is the adversary the draft is tested against. <!-- prevents: the rival vanishing once the tree is built; evidence: decision D2 (the rival as an adversarial review after the research), design step 11 -->
- Settle the terms. <!-- prevents: an ambiguous term resolved by widening or never resolved; evidence: decision D3, run 1 diagnosis point A2 -->
- Write what each key term of the question means and, in one clause, why the definition matters. <!-- prevents: a definition that changes the count by a multiple left unstated or unexplained (ports, chargers and stations in run 1); evidence: decision D3, run 1 diagnosis D-6 -->
- When a term has two reasonable readings and both are cheap to measure, research both and say which one the stated target or its source uses. <!-- prevents: one reading picked silently when the other was cheap to show; evidence: decision D3 (run 1's "ports" and "stations"), run 1 diagnosis D-6 -->
- Ask the user only when the readings would change the whole research; otherwise pick one and say so. <!-- prevents: a run blocked on a question nobody will answer, and a choice of reading hidden from the reader; evidence: decision D3, design steps 1 and 16 -->
- Choose the top cut deliberately. <!-- prevents: the first cut that came to mind becoming the tree; evidence: the earlier method step 3 -->
- Try two or three ways to split the question: by the equation that produces the result, by the stages of a process or funnel, by competing causes, or by independent lines of evidence. <!-- prevents: a cut chosen without alternatives, and options offered as a cut (run 1); evidence: the earlier method step 3 and its source-logic router, decision D2 -->
- For each, say what it reveals and what it hides. <!-- prevents: a cut whose blind spot nobody named; evidence: the earlier method step 3 -->
- Pick the cut that would make a wrong answer easiest to disconfirm, and say why. <!-- prevents: a cut chosen for tidiness rather than for testing the answer; evidence: the earlier method's router, "pick the one that would make a wrong answer easiest to disconfirm" -->
- Some fits recur: a profit or cost question puts its controlling equation at the root; a sizing question starts from the population or customer funnel and converts to value afterwards; a question whether a named change would work is tested on what must hold for it to. <!-- prevents: the commonest mis-cuts the earlier router named (an equation root for a proposal that feasibility can kill, revenue before the funnel); evidence: the earlier method's tree-type and source-logic routers -->
- One division principle per sibling group, at every depth. <!-- prevents: overlap and gaps below the top level, where the earlier method found exclusivity "quietly dies"; evidence: the earlier method step 4 -->
- State it in one line where the group appears. <!-- prevents: a principle held in the head and lost in the tree; evidence: the earlier method step 4 -->
- Siblings do not overlap and together cover their parent question; say what the group leaves out. <!-- prevents: double-counted effects and silently missing branches; evidence: the earlier method step 4, P3 R4 -->
- When siblings can interact, state the rule that assigns each effect to exactly one of them. <!-- prevents: one effect counted under two branches; evidence: the earlier method step 4, run 1 diagnosis D-2 (the pace and change rule kept its cut clean) -->
- Deep levels are where overlap creeps in, so the deeper the group, the more explicit its principle. <!-- prevents: discipline held only at the top level; evidence: the earlier method step 4 -->
- Draw depth as sub-questions. <!-- prevents: depth written as methods inside nodes, invisible to every reader of the tree (run 1); evidence: decision D2, run 1 diagnosis cause C4 and D-1 -->
- Each node is a question with how doubtful its answer is, what would settle it (the measurable variable or observable stand-in, and where it would come from) and the answer at which the headline would change. <!-- prevents: research with no stopping point and no link to the answer; evidence: design step 3, contract section 5 tree fields, the earlier method steps 5 and 7 -->
- How a node will be answered becomes its child questions, such as which official series and on what basis, or what counts and what does not, never notes inside the node. <!-- prevents: the analysis plan hidden in "settled by" prose and work orders, so the trouble over which count to use surfaced late (run 1); evidence: decision D2 (its "today's count" example), run 1 diagnosis cause C4 -->
- No option, rival, gate, independent check or work plan is a node; keep one open lane for primary-source reading nobody planned that bears on the question. <!-- prevents: one structure holding six different things, so the tree reads as broken (run 1's O1 to O5, R1, TB and T0); the unplanned reading that most changed answers having no home; evidence: decision D2, run 1 diagnosis cause C3 and the node table; lesson 4.3, design step 3 -->
- A parent states what its children together imply, not a category label. <!-- prevents: "intellectually blank" summaries that hide unfinished thinking; evidence: the earlier method step 4, D1 principle 2 -->
- Rank, set aside, then order. <!-- prevents: every node treated as equal, everything dispatched at once (run 1's five workers at 07:22); evidence: decision D5, run 1 diagnosis cause C5 -->
- Rank nodes by how much each could change the headline and how doubtful it is, and mark the one to three the answer turns on. <!-- prevents: effort placed by ease rather than by what could change the answer; evidence: the earlier method step 6, design step 3 -->
- Set a node aside only when neither of its possible answers could change the headline, and record why on the node; a node that is merely hard to answer is never set aside. <!-- prevents: irrelevant branches protected by "never drop", without reopening the older failure of a hard, decisive branch dropped because it was hard; evidence: decision D5, run 1 diagnosis cause C5 and point A12, lesson 4.3 -->
- Branches run in parallel unless one's value depends on another's answer; then hold it, and say so in its order. <!-- prevents: work spent on a branch an earlier answer would have made moot, without serialising branches that are independent; evidence: decision D5, run 1 diagnosis point A13 -->
- Group leaves into lines of evidence. <!-- prevents: one worker per leaf, splitting a single source across agents; evidence: design step 4, P1 principle 2 -->
- Leaves that rest on the same sources become one line of work; the line, not the leaf, is what one worker takes. <!-- prevents: duplicated reading and lost cross-leaf detail; evidence: design step 4 -->
- Check, and revise once if a check fails. <!-- prevents: a tree approved in under a minute without a look back; evidence: lesson 4.3, the earlier method step 8 -->
- Is every node part of the question asked, and is any part of it missing? <!-- prevents: no step anywhere asking "is this the question that was asked?" (run 1); evidence: decision D1 and decision D2, run 1 diagnosis cause C9 -->
- Is each division principle stated, and is the separation real? <!-- prevents: overlap just below the top that the root hides; evidence: the earlier method step 8 -->
- Would settling the crux settle the answer? <!-- prevents: a crux that does not decide anything; evidence: the earlier method step 8 -->
- What finding would force a re-cut rather than a re-weighting? <!-- prevents: a tree defended after the evidence has broken it; evidence: the earlier method step 8, design step 10 -->
- Write that last answer into frame.md. <!-- prevents: the re-cut trigger forgotten when the finding arrives; evidence: design step 10 -->
- Use no stock framework (SWOT, five forces, the four Ps) unless it fits this question, and then say why. <!-- prevents: a borrowed framework that would fit any question; evidence: the earlier method's guardrails, P3 failure kind 14 -->
- Give no branch equal depth for symmetry. <!-- prevents: depth spread for appearance; evidence: the earlier method's guardrails -->
- The tree states what must be answered; the orders say who looks where, so keep the two separate and let leaves change without re-approving the top. <!-- prevents: a tree frozen into a work plan, so downstream findings cannot amend the leaves; evidence: design step 3, lesson 4.3 -->

## card-company-evaluation

- Use this card when the question is about a company; it says where to look and what to ask, and it holds no industry knowledge and no valuation method. <!-- prevents: a company lens applied to every question, and a card standing in for domain knowledge the user should attach; evidence: lessons 2.0, 2.0a and 2.2a -->
- For a pitch, the goal is insight the company's own management may lack, grounded in its record, not a price for the company. <!-- prevents: a pitch answered with a valuation or a summary of what is already public; evidence: lesson 2.2a, the owner's brief for this card -->
- Search the company's own published record for what the scoped question needs (its site and documents, product, pricing, terms and hiring pages, leadership interviews), and read whole every document you rely on; a sample of a page is not the page. <!-- prevents: findings drawn from a sampled page, without ordering every document a company publishes whatever the question; evidence: lesson 2.2a item 1, decision D1, Codex review of the v2 texts -->
- Where a change over time bears on the question, compare its statements through dated archived copies. <!-- prevents: missing the change that its press releases do not mention; evidence: lesson 2.2a item 1 and its invented example -->
- Where the question needs them, go on to its regulatory filings, the records any company leaves (registries, officer filings, secured-lending filings, court records, licences, patents and trademarks, government contracts), and the full disclosures of its public customers, suppliers, competitors and acquirers. <!-- prevents: a private company treated as having no record, and peers' disclosures left unread, without widening a narrow question into a full company study; evidence: lesson 2.2a items 2 to 4, decision D1, Codex review of the v2 texts -->
- Use official statistics at source and recompute from the files; use trade press and reviews as leads and as the market's voice. <!-- prevents: a write-up's figure taken in place of the data it summarises; evidence: lesson 2.2a items 5 to 7 -->
- Each of these is a prompt to look, not a quota: market definition and size, position against a named universe of competitors, where the profit is earned against peers' disclosed unit economics, customer and supplier concentration counted at the controlling group, channel, ownership and management, key contracts and regulatory exposure, technology and operating model. <!-- prevents: a checklist filled for its own sake, and concentration understated by counting subsidiaries separately; evidence: lesson 2.2a -->
- For any of these that turns on how the business works, model the mechanism from data (see card-mechanism) rather than quote someone's conclusion. <!-- prevents: insight borrowed from analysts instead of built; evidence: lessons 1.2 and 2.11 -->
- Record which lenses you ran, which returned something, and which you skipped and why. <!-- prevents: silent gaps in coverage; evidence: lesson 2.2a -->
- Tier the company's description of itself as interested_party until a count or a primary source confirms it. <!-- prevents: a company's own count of itself carried as fact (an invented example, labelled as invented: a firm's site says "more than 300 branches" and the memo repeats it as a measured count); evidence: lesson 2.4 -->
- State an inference as a finding with its basis and confidence, separate from what was measured, and make it a question only where the evidence in hand cannot carry it. <!-- prevents: claiming knowledge the research lacks, without turning defensible insights into questions; evidence: lesson 7 rule 7, blind review of these texts -->
- When the client's own questions are known, map each to answered, out of scope by agreement, or unaddressed. <!-- prevents: most of a client's questions never assigned; evidence: lesson 2.8 -->

## card-decision

- Use this card when the question itself asks whether to act or which option to take; it holds method, not an industry's economics or a valuation model. <!-- prevents: a decision lens applied to every question, including one whose purpose line merely mentions a decision (run 1 loaded it as the main card for a fact question), and a generic framework filling a specialist gap; evidence: lessons 2.0 and 2.0a, decision D2, run 1 diagnosis "Where it came from" -->
- Take the options the question or the user names: for "whether to do X", doing it and not doing it; for "A or B", A and B. <!-- prevents: options invented at framing that the user never posed (run 1's O1 to O5), without losing the alternative a "whether" question already contains; evidence: decision D2 (options fixed at framing when the user names them), run 1 diagnosis point A5 -->
- Make the tree's sub-questions the criteria every option is compared on, so that no option owns a subtree, and find early which criterion could end the question. <!-- prevents: one nested tree per option, and effort spread evenly while the knock-out criteria wait; evidence: decision D2, run 1 diagnosis D-11, P3 failure kind 12 -->
- If the question asks what to do and names no options, work the options out from the findings after the research, never before it. <!-- prevents: options placed before research shaping which nodes get researched; evidence: decision D2, run 1 diagnosis D-11 and point A7 (levers read off the drivers the research found) -->
- Take what the decider is trying to achieve from the question and task/; if the options cannot be ranked against it, say so as the first finding. <!-- prevents: options compared with each other instead of with the goal, without turning the purpose into research of its own; evidence: D1 principle 12, decision D1, blind review of the v2 texts -->
- Go to the primary material about the thing being decided: for a physical asset, the records of whoever grants its permits and connections; for a company, its record and its counterparties'; for a product, the people who would buy it. <!-- prevents: a decision made from articles about the sector; evidence: lesson 2.2 and its invented example -->
- Treat the materials of anyone selling the deal or the venture as advocacy, tiered interested_party, and look for what they leave out. <!-- prevents: a seller's figures taken as fact; evidence: lesson 2.4 -->
- Quantify the effect of each option with its assumptions and a range, and say at what value of the deciding fact the choice would flip. <!-- prevents: a comparison with no magnitude or break-even; evidence: D1 principle 12, P3 R2 -->
- Recommend an option only when the question asks for a recommendation, and then state what would have to happen for it to fail and whether any of it is already visible. <!-- prevents: a recommendation nobody asked for (run 1's spending headline), and one that has never been argued against; evidence: decision D2 (under-prescribe), run 1 diagnosis point A3, D1 principle 12, lesson 7 rule 12 -->
- Give the reason for any choice as how it meets the goal, with the real alternatives shown and straw men left out. <!-- prevents: a choice justified by knocking down options nobody proposed; evidence: D1 principle 12 -->

## card-market-sizing

- Use this card when the question asks how large a market or opportunity is; it holds method, not figures for any industry. <!-- prevents: a sizing lens forced onto questions that do not need it, and remembered figures standing in for sources; evidence: lessons 2.0 and 2.0a -->
- Define the market by the customer need and what substitutes for it, not by an industry label, starting from the user's own term, and state the definition before any number. <!-- prevents: a size that cannot be compared or checked because its scope is unstated; evidence: lessons 3.0 and 7 rule 4 -->
- Size it bottom-up from a funnel (the population that has the need, those who can be reached, those who adopt, at what price and frequency) and top-down from totals such as official statistics, sector filings or peers' revenues. <!-- prevents: a single route whose error nobody can see; evidence: lesson 2.2a "sized both top-down and bottom-up" -->
- Where adoption, access or awareness binds, put it in the funnel as its own stage rather than inside an adoption rate. <!-- prevents: the binding constraint hidden in one assumed percentage; evidence: the earlier method's sizing router, lesson 2.11 step 1 -->
- Reconcile the two routes, and check the share a known player's revenue implies; a large gap means a definition or an input is wrong, and is investigated rather than averaged. <!-- prevents: averaging away the error that would have shown the definition was wrong; evidence: lesson 2.11 step 4 -->
- Take official statistics from the source files and recompute; tier vendor and association totals as interested_party or secondary. <!-- prevents: a report's market size quoted as measured; evidence: lessons 2.2a item 5 and 2.4 -->
- Build the calculation in a script in analysis/ with each input labelled observed, estimated or assumed, and show which two or three inputs move the answer most and the range they imply. <!-- prevents: a point estimate with no basis or range; evidence: lesson 2.11, P3 R2 -->
- A narrow sizing question gets a short answer with its method, not a deck. <!-- prevents: a one-number task padded into a long deliverable; evidence: lesson 7D "When it applies", design step 13 -->

## card-mechanism
- Use this card when the answer turns on how something works or what a change would do; you build the analysis rather than look for a source that states the answer. <!-- prevents: hunting for a headline that states the answer; evidence: lesson 2.11, design step 7 -->
- Write the output as an equation of its drivers before collecting any number. <!-- prevents: inputs gathered with no structure to say which matter; evidence: lesson 2.11 step 1 -->
- Label each input observed, estimated or assumed, with its source and the basis it is on (the price actually paid or the list price, one period or another). <!-- prevents: an assumed input presented as sourced, and inputs on mismatched bases; evidence: lesson 2.11 step 2, lesson 3.0 on basis -->
- Compute it in a script in analysis/, and show the break-even and the range over the inputs that move it most. <!-- prevents: arithmetic done in prose, and a point answer with no sense of what moves it; evidence: lesson 2.11 step 3 -->
- For example, a price cut pays only if volume rises enough to replace the margin it gives up, and the script shows how much that is. <!-- prevents: a price change recommended without its break-even, the owner's named example of a mechanism question; evidence: lesson 2.11 and its invented example, the brief for this card -->
- Test the model against what was observed (past changes, reported results, peers), and investigate a large gap rather than averaging it away. <!-- prevents: a tidy model that history already contradicts; evidence: lesson 2.11 step 4 -->
- Say how competitors or customers could react, and whether the result holds if they do. <!-- prevents: a change that only works if nobody responds; evidence: lesson 2.11, D1 principle 12 -->
- Say when the method is specialist (pricing studies, actuarial or engineering models), use an attached study or ask for one, and otherwise label the method first-principles. <!-- prevents: a specialist question answered with a generic model presented as expert; evidence: lessons 2.0a and 2.11 step 5 -->
