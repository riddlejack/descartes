# Card: the double bass

The double bass is the largest and lowest of the bowed strings in the orchestra, and players stand or sit on a high stool to reach it.

## An instrument of two families

The double bass shares features with the older viol family as well as with the violin: many have sloping shoulders, and some have a flat back. It is usually tuned in fourths rather than fifths, so that the hand can reach the notes on its long strings, and some have a fifth string or an extension for the lowest notes.

## Bows and players

Two kinds of bow are in use: the French bow, held like a cello bow, and the German bow, held from below with a deeper frog. In the nineteenth century Domenico Dragonetti and Giovanni Bottesini toured as virtuosos. In jazz the strings are mostly plucked rather than bowed.

## In ensembles

In the orchestra the basses stand at the back or to one side, often doubling the cellos an octave lower. A full section may have eight players. The instrument is just as much at home in jazz bands, folk groups and early rock and roll, where players slap the strings against the fingerboard for a percussive sound. Smaller basses are made for children, and electric versions without a hollow body are common on stage.
