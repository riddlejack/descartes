# Card: the cello

The cello is the tenor and bass voice of the violin family, held between the knees and tuned an octave below the viola.

## Early forms

The first cellos, made in Italy in the sixteenth century, were often larger than the modern instrument and went by several names. Around the start of the eighteenth century Antonio Stradivari settled on a somewhat smaller pattern, which most later makers followed. Early players held the cello off the floor with their legs, and the endpin came into common use only in the nineteenth century. Its strings were long made of gut, and the lowest two were later wound with fine wire to give them a fuller, rounder voice.

## Music for the cello

For a long time the cello mainly played the bass line in church and chamber music. Bach's six suites for unaccompanied cello stand apart, though they were little played until Pablo Casals took them up and recorded them in the 1930s. Players still argue over how fast the dances in them should go.

## Players and composers

Luigi Boccherini, himself a cellist, wrote many concertos and sonatas for it. Later composers, from Dvořák to Elgar, wrote concertos that made it a favourite solo instrument. Its range runs from deep bass notes to a high, singing register.

In the orchestra the cellos sit near the front, often at the conductor's right, and carry many of the long, singing tunes. A full section may number eight to twelve players, and the principal cellist plays the solos that composers write into symphonies and ballets. Cellos also appear widely outside classical music, in folk, rock and jazz groups and in film scores.
