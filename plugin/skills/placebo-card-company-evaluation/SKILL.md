# Card: the viola

The viola is the alto voice of the violin family, a little larger than the violin and tuned a fifth lower. Its music is written mostly in the alto clef, which few other instruments use.

## Size and shape

Violins settled on one standard size long ago, but violas never did. Their bodies vary by several centimetres from one maker to the next, because a body large enough for the lowest string to speak freely is awkward to hold under the chin. Makers have long tried to balance a full low register against a size that players can manage. Some of the old Italian violas were very large and were later cut down to a more comfortable length.

## Makers and players

Andrea Amati and his family made violas, and Antonio Stradivari made a handful, of which only about ten survive. For most of the eighteenth century the viola filled in the harmony of orchestras and string quartets. Mozart and Haydn both enjoyed playing it in quartets with friends, and Mozart gave it a leading part in his Sinfonia Concertante for violin and viola.

## A solo voice

The nineteenth century gave the viola a few solo roles, among them Berlioz's Harold in Italy. In the twentieth century the English violist Lionel Tertis and the Scottish violist William Primrose built careers as soloists and persuaded composers to write for them. Walton wrote a concerto for the instrument, and Bartók left another unfinished at his death; it was completed later by his friend Tibor Serly. Today the viola has a large solo repertoire of its own. Hindemith, himself a fine violist, wrote sonatas and a concerto for it, and many living composers have followed him with new works for players to learn.

## In the orchestra

In a modern symphony orchestra the violas usually sit between the second violins and the cellos, or on the outside to the conductor's right. A full orchestra may carry ten or twelve of them. Their part often doubles the cellos an octave higher or fills in the middle of the chords, and their darker tone blends easily with horns and clarinets. Composers from Brahms onward gave the section long melodies of its own, and some wrote solo passages for the principal violist.
