# Building the issue tree

The tree breaks the question asked into the sub-questions whose answers, together, answer it. Your reasoning about the question (its terms, the working answer and its rival, why you chose the top cut) goes in frame.md; the tree holds only the questions.

1. **Ideas.** Start from framer/ideas.md and add your own: every driver, factor and sub-question an expert would raise. Every idea either finds a place in the tree or is set aside with its reason in frame.md; none is dropped silently. A placed idea is a node of its own, a child of the node it is a part or a cause of, never folded into another node's wording or settles_by. Only set-aside ideas leave the tree.

2. **Structure.** Take the top cut from how the situation works, not from the question's wording: split a whole into its parts, trace the causes of one result, or sort like things into classes. Try two or three cuts, keep the most revealing, and say why in frame.md. Each parent's children come from one of those acts, are the same kind of question, and sit one step below it: answered together they answer the parent, and no two answer the same thing. A factor that reaches the parent only through one child goes under that child; one acting through several goes inside each, as that child's own question. Test each group: one specific plural noun names it, and its members fall in one order (parts by structure, causes by time, classes by importance). More than five members usually hides a subgroup. Use the structure to find what the idea list missed. Split a branch until evidence can settle it.

3. **Prune.** Set aside only ideas whose every answer leaves the headline unchanged, each named in frame.md with its reason. No branch rests on an unchecked premise: make the premise the question. No node asks for a definition, a method, a sensitivity or a work plan.

**Every node.** Each node is one short question with one idea, in plain words; how to answer it goes in settles_by. A parent does not list or summarise its children, and a node has no children or at least two. The tree holds only sub-questions of the question asked: no option, rival, check or work plan is a node. For a decision among named options, the top parts are what the choice turns on, asked of every option alike; for a question asking which options are best, the first part finds the candidates. Keep one open_lane node for primary reading nobody planned. Only leaves need doubt, settles_by and flip_point. Mark the one to three nodes the answer turns on as crux. Leaves that rest on the same sources share one branch_id: a branch, not a leaf, is one worker's job. When a check or a critique finds a gap, an overlap or a node doing two jobs, add, split, move or merge nodes; never fix a tree by adding words to a node.
